## what-happened

This page is a layout exercise, not an account of a decision published by a government. In the sample, an economic team studies a rule that would limit how far some public expenses can grow above inflation. Inflation is the general rise in prices. Growth above inflation is what is left after that rise is taken out. The fictional conversation names health and education among the areas. No bill, decree, or vote is attached to this text.

## in-plain-terms

A cap of this kind is a limit on the increase in one year. If prices rise 4 percent and the cap allows 1 percent above inflation, the expense could grow by about 5 percent in that year. The sample does not set a real percentage. It only shows how the mechanism would be explained, with a short sum and without predicting a cut.

## who-may-be-affected

In a real debate, the people who use public health and education services would be the groups named. This sample does not say who would gain or lose money, because no rule has been written. An estimate of a cut would be a guess, and this page does not make that guess.

## where-things-stand

The stage is under discussion. That means the idea is being talked about. It is not in force and, in this sample, it has not been introduced as a formal proposal. The next step would be a written text. Nothing here starts that step. The question “is this already in force?” has an answer the sample can confirm: no.
