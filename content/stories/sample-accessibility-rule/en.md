## what-happened

This page is a sample. In it, a fictional assembly in Brazil approves a rule on accessible service at in-person public counters. Here, approved means the floor voted for the text. Disabled people are people who meet barriers to taking part on equal terms. The sample text asks for a Brazilian Sign Language interpreter when the service is booked ahead of time. Brazilian Sign Language is the sign language used in Brazil, often called Libras. The vote described here did not happen outside this page.

## in-plain-terms

The mechanism is a duty of the public office, not a favor. If a person books the visit and asks for Libras, the office in the sample would have to provide an interpreter at the booked time. In this fiction the rule does not create a cash benefit. It changes how the service is delivered.

## who-may-be-affected

The direct group in the sample is deaf people who use Libras and need an in-person public service. Staff who keep the calendar would also have a new task: record the request and call the interpreter. The sample does not measure how many people would be reached, and it does not treat that reach as a figure already counted.

## where-things-stand

The stage is approved. The fictional floor has already voted. The rule is not in force yet, because the sample itself says regulation is still missing. Regulation is the text that explains how the rule will be carried out day to day. The expected next step is that publication. Until then, the approval does not change real service.
